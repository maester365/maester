  # Research digest: making configuration-assessment reports more insightful (as of 2026-11-05)
  
  Sources are mostly primary vendor docs and papers. Figures that come from vendors are labelled. **[Spec]** marks my own inference.
  
  ## 1. Attack paths, toxic combinations and exposure graphs
  
  - **Microsoft Security Exposure Management (MSEM)** builds attack paths from the *enterprise exposure graph*: "end-to-end paths… from an entry point… to critical assets". **Choke points** are nodes where several paths converge, and **blast radius** shows what one compromised asset can reach. The dashboard ranks top choke points, scenarios, targets and entry points. Paths are generated only for imminent, exploitable threats, and Microsoft warns they are incomplete if critical assets aren't defined. [MS Learn: attack paths](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-exposure-management/work-attack-paths-overview)
  - **MSEM critical asset management** uses predefined classifiers (for example the Privileged Role Administrator role and Power Users groups), custom queries and manual tagging, with a criticality level. Assets classified "with lower confidence" go to a human for review. Criticality then feeds attack paths and hunting. [MS Learn: critical assets](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-exposure-management/critical-asset-management)
  - **Wiz** defines a critical Issue as a *toxic combination*: "a very high likelihood of being compromised" plus "significant business impact". The inputs are exposure, vulnerabilities, excessive permissions and sensitive data, all joined in one graph that is "the database, the normalizing data model, and the analysis layer". The scoring formula isn't public. [Wiz blog](https://www.wiz.io/blog/the-anatomy-of-a-toxic-combination-of-risk)
  - **Google Security Command Center** documents the most explicit scoring: an *attack exposure score* computed from the number and priority of exposed high-value resources and the likelihood an attacker reaches them. A score ≥10 is Critical, and chokepoints are always Critical. Simulations run about every 48 hours and findings auto-close once fixed. [Google SCC docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/security-command-center/docs/toxic-combinations-overview)
  - **BloodHound** defines an attack path as a chain of abusable privileges between principals. *Exposure* is the count and percentage of principals that have a path to Tier Zero, and a choke point is the best place to cut the most paths ([glossary](https://bloodhound.specterops.io/resources/glossary/overview)). *Privilege Zones* tag Tier Zero by rule or Cypher query and add any object that controls a tagged object. Since v7.6, principals that are PIM-eligible for Tier Zero roles are tagged too ([Privilege Zones](https://bloodhound.specterops.io/analyze-data/privilege-zones/overview)).
  - **XM Cyber** (vendor research) reports that about 74–75% of exposures are "dead ends" that can't reach critical assets, and only about 2% sit on choke points. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/most-attack-paths-are-dead-ends-but-2-lead-to-critical-assets-report/)
  - **Tenable Identity Exposure** is the closest analogue to Maester: fixed-severity Entra "Indicators of Exposure", for example "MFA Not Required for a Privileged Role" rated High ([IoE](https://www.tenable.com/indicators/ioe/entra/MFA-NOT-REQUIRED-FOR-A-PRIVILEGED-ROLE)). **ROADtools** dumps Entra into a local SQLite database through SQLAlchemy and has plugins for a Conditional Access HTML report and BloodHound export ([repo](https://github.com/dirkjanm/ROADtools)). I could not find a project called "EntraHound".
  - **Pattern:** a property graph of principals, roles, apps and resources, with edges for privilege and control. Severity comes from reachability to a critical set multiplied by likelihood, and choke points are the nodes many paths to that set pass through.
  
  ## 2. Prioritisation beyond severity
  
  - **SSVC (CISA):** a decision tree over *Exploitation* (none/PoC/active), *Automatable*, *Technical impact* (partial/total) and *Mission prevalence*, ending in Track, Track*, Attend or Act. [CISA SSVC guide](https://us-cert.gov/sites/default/files/publications/cisa-ssvc-guide%20508c.pdf) **[Spec]** It carries over well to misconfigurations: exploitation can come from active use of the mapped ATT&CK technique, automatable means sprayable at scale, and total impact means tenant takeover. Mission prevalence becomes critical-asset involvement.
  - **EPSS** gives the probability a CVE is exploited within 30 days and is keyed to CVEs ([FIRST](https://www.first.org/epss/)). It does not apply directly. **[Spec]** The idea that does carry over is a prior on likelihood drawn from incident data per ATT&CK technique.
  - **Microsoft Secure Score:** each action is worth 10 points or fewer, mostly scored binary with some partial credit. Ranking uses points left, difficulty, user impact and complexity, and risk-accepted actions drop out of the denominator ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-xdr/microsoft-secure-score-improvement-actions)). It compares you with organisations of similar seat count and with a custom comparison set ([history and comparison](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-xdr/microsoft-secure-score-history-metrics-trends)).
  - **MSEM initiatives** (for example Ransomware protection and BEC) group recommendations into metrics, and **the admin can change each metric's weight** to reflect business priority. This means one finding set gets different rankings for different goals. [MS Learn: initiatives](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security-exposure-management/initiatives)
  - **Applicability to configuration findings:** choke-point and blast-radius scoring, critical-asset weighting, SSVC-style trees and adjustable goal weights all apply. EPSS does not, except as an analogy.
  
  ## 3. LLM-generated security reporting
  
  - **Security Copilot promptbooks** are chained prompts in which each step builds on the last and ends in a readable report. Microsoft tells users to verify the output. [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/copilot/security/using-promptbooks)
  - **Conditional Access optimization agent:** every 24 hours it checks new users, apps and agent identities for CA coverage and suggests policies. By default it creates them in **report-only** mode, and admins approve changes and phased rollout. It requires Entra ID P1. [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/conditional-access/agent-optimization) Security Copilot is now included with M365 E5, with Security Compute Units (SCU) capacity scaled to seat count. [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/security/security-copilot-inclusion)
  - **Microsoft Zero Trust Assessment** has no LLM features. Each test carries risk level, user impact and implementation effort, plus a deep link to fix it. Analysis runs locally, and Microsoft says to treat the output as "highly sensitive". [Get started](https://learn.microsoft.com/security/zero-trust/assessment/get-started), [Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/microsoft-security-community/the-microsoft-zero-trust-assessment-helping-you-operationalize/ba-p/4468729)
  - **Tenable ExposureAI** writes a summary for each attack path, answers follow-up questions about the path or its nodes, and gives mitigation guidance that says how many paths a fix closes. It runs on Gemini in Vertex AI. [Press release](https://www.tenable.com/press-releases/tenable-expands-generative-ai-capabilities-for-faster-attack-path-analysis-and-mitigation-guidance)
  - **Failure modes:** in tests of three LLMs on 350 threat-intelligence reports, the models were inconsistent and *overconfident*, and few-shot prompting or fine-tuning helped only partly ([Mezzi et al., arXiv 2503.23175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23175)). A related paper on threat research also reports invented indicators of compromise and wrong ATT&CK labels.
  - **Mitigation evidence:** DeepFaith links every statement to an evidence ID, uses faithfulness-aware generation and verifies after generation. Faithfulness rose from 0.68 to 0.92 and unsupported claims fell from 0.32 to 0.08 ([arXiv 2607.24348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24348)). In practice that means structured input, mandatory citations to finding or object IDs, and a deterministic check that rejects any claim without a valid reference.
  
  ## 4. Persona- and goal-based reporting
  
  - **NIST CSF 2.0** Organizational Profiles compare a Current Profile with a Target Profile to find gaps ([SP 1301](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1301.pdf)). Tiers describe how rigorous governance is and inform the target ([SP 1302](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1302.pdf)).
  - **CIS Implementation Groups** are cumulative: IG1 has 56 safeguards ("essential cyber hygiene"), IG2 brings the total to 130 and IG3 to 153. [CIS](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/implementation-groups)
  - **ASD Essential Eight** has maturity levels ML0–ML3 tied to *adversary tradecraft and targeting*. There's no required level: you pick a target from the threat you expect. [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/resources-business-and-government/essential-cyber-security/essential-eight/essential-eight-maturity-model)
  - **MSEM initiatives** and **Secure Score comparisons** (above) are the shipping examples of goal-based re-weighting and peer context.
  - **[Spec]** I found no product that asks "why are you reading this?" and re-ranks. Asking for the goal (audit, post-incident, first hardening, M&A) and mapping it to a framework target (CIS IG, E8 ML, CSF Target Profile) plus a weight preset looks like open ground.
  
  ## 5. "System One" fast decision models and embedding classifiers
  
  - **hunch** ([undrgrnd-labs/hunch](https://github.com/undrgrnd-labs/hunch)) is a proxy that distils a teacher (an LLM judge) into a small classifier per question, trained on EmbeddingGemma 2 embeddings of your own records. A question "graduates" only once cross-validation shows it agrees with the teacher, and it keeps auditing against the teacher and sends out-of-distribution inputs back to it. It runs on CPU without a GPU, at about 22 ms p50 on an M5 Pro. Licence is MIT/Apache-2.0. The repo says it's a poor fit for questions that need heavy reasoning.
  - **TypeSafe Jev** (released 2026-09-15) and **Cloudflare Clef / Clef-flash** (2026-10-01, open weights under Apache-2.0, compatible with the Jev API) return typed answers (yes/no, choice, score) with calibrated probabilities instead of text. The benchmarks are vendor-reported and conflict between sources, and Clef is reported at about 27B parameters. [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/01/cloudflare-releases-clef-and-clef-flash/)
  - **SetFit** fine-tunes a sentence-transformer contrastively on a handful of labelled pairs and then trains a small head. It is competitive with parameter-efficient fine-tuning and much faster, with no prompts. [arXiv 2209.11055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11055)
  - **AlertBERT** clusters alert embeddings with a time-aware distance to group alerts and cut analyst fatigue. [arXiv 2602.06534](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06534v1)
  - **Fit for Maester [Spec]:** check outcomes are deterministic, so a classifier shouldn't decide pass or fail. The fit is for *judgement over free text and names*: whether an account looks like break-glass, test or service; grouping CA policies by intent; clustering affected objects; triaging sign-in and audit rows. The pattern that suits offline use is an embedding model plus a tiny head, with no network call.
  
  ## 6. Local and offline AI, MCP and WebMCP
  
  - **EmbeddingGemma** has 308M parameters, runs in under 200 MB RAM quantized, and supports Matryoshka truncation of its 768-dimension output to 512, 256 or 128. It works with Transformers.js, ONNX and llama.cpp. [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/embeddinggemma)
  - **Transformers.js v3** enables WebGPU with `device: 'webgpu'` through ONNX Runtime Web. WebGPU is on by default in Chrome and Edge 113+, and still behind flags in Firefox and Safari. [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/transformersjs-v3) **ONNX Runtime GenAI** runs Phi and Gemma on-device across Windows, macOS and Linux. [GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime-genai)
  - **Microsoft MCP Server for Enterprise** is in preview, read-only, with three tools (suggest queries, list properties, GET). It honours roles and scopes, is limited to 100 calls/min per user, and has no extra cost. [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/graph/mcp-server/overview) **Lokka** is community-built, runs locally, and covers all of Graph plus ARM, including writes.
  - **WebMCP** is a W3C Web ML CG incubation that lets a page register tools through `navigator.modelContext` (newer drafts use `document.modelContext`). Secondary sources say it reached a Chrome origin trial around v149, with Gemini in Chrome as the only consumer so far, and that tools on HTTP pages aren't exposed. [Spec repo](https://github.com/webmachinelearning/webmcp) **[Spec]** A report opened from `file://` may not qualify, so check before designing around it.
  - **Privacy:** the report is itself sensitive tenant data (see the Zero Trust Assessment guidance). Local inference keeps the "nothing leaves the session" promise, while any cloud LLM summary needs explicit opt-in and redaction.
  
  ## 7. Inferring tenant context from data
  
  - Entra marks roles **`isPrivileged`** when they contain a sensitive `microsoft.directory` action. That gives you Tier Zero roles without a hand-kept list. [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/privileged-roles-permissions)
  - **Break-glass fingerprint** from Microsoft's guidance: two or more cloud-only `*.onmicrosoft.com` accounts, Global Administrator **permanent active** (not PIM-eligible), FIDO2 or certificate-based sign-in that differs from other admins, excluded from blocking CA policies (often through a dedicated group), and rare sign-ins. [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/security-emergency-access)
  - **Prior art for "classify, then confirm":** MSEM's classifiers with a human review queue for low-confidence results, BloodHound's rule-based Tier Zero plus PIM-eligible tagging, and Secure Score's peer groups by seat count. All three are linked above.
  - **[Spec]** Other signals Maester could read: hybrid vs cloud-only from `organization.onPremisesSyncEnabled` and federated domains; licence tier from `subscribedSkus`; size from user and device counts; maturity proxies such as PIM, CA and phishing-resistant MFA adoption; critical apps from privileged Graph app permissions. Industry is hard to infer and is better asked.
  
  ## Ideas most transferable to a configuration-assessment report (ranked by value/effort)
  
  1. **Inferred tenant profile card**: size, hybrid, licences, privileged-user count and break-glass candidates, each with a confidence and a "confirm/correct" control. *Low effort, high value.*
  2. **Tier Zero tagging of affected objects**: use `isPrivileged`, PIM-eligible and high-risk app permissions to sort affected objects and boost findings that touch Tier Zero. *Low effort.*
  3. **A transparent "fix-first" score**: Severity × critical-asset involvement ÷ ImplementationCost, with a "why ranked here" tooltip, all built on attributes Maester already has. *Low effort.*
  4. **Goal lenses** (audit, post-incident, first hardening, M&A due diligence) as weight presets over the same results, like MSEM initiatives, each tied to a CIS IG, E8 ML or CSF target. *Low–medium effort.*
  5. **Declarative toxic-combination rules**: co-failing checks (for example, legacy auth allowed + admins without phishing-resistant MFA + standing Global Admins) rolled into one Critical "combination" finding that cites its member test IDs. *Medium effort, high value.*
  6. **SSVC-style Act/Attend/Track** for failed checks, using technique exploitation, automatable, impact and critical-asset involvement. *Medium effort.*
  7. **Regression storytelling**: put changes since the last run that touch Tier Zero or combination rules first. *Low effort, given the trend data Maester already stores.*
  8. **Deterministic narrative first, optional LLM summary second**: the summary takes structured JSON in, must cite a test or object ID for every claim, and a verifier rejects anything unsupported. *Medium effort.*
  9. **Identity choke-point view**: a small graph of role assignments, app owners, app permissions and group membership to show "fixing X removes N paths to Tier Zero". *High effort, high value.*
  10. **In-browser semantic search and clustering** of findings and affected objects with EmbeddingGemma through Transformers.js and WebGPU, fully offline. *Medium effort.*
  11. **Agent surface for the report**: an MCP or WebMCP tool set (query results, explain a test, list Tier Zero). *Medium effort.* **[Spec]** WebMCP is still an origin trial and may need HTTPS hosting.
  12. **A distilled local classifier** (hunch- or SetFit-style) for naming and intent judgements (break-glass, test or service accounts; CA policy intent). *High effort.* **[Spec]** Value depends on whether enough labelled data exists.
